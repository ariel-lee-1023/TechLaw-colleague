# Move 3 — doctrinal-mechanics diagnosis (depth)

*Source capacity: Lemley — Law, Virtual Reality, and Augmented Reality (Lemley & Volokh, 2018);
The Patent Crisis and How the Courts Can Solve It (Burk & Lemley, 2009). Load for the doctrinal step:
which category the new facts strain, what a rule is load-bearing on, and how to tailor without new
statutes.*

## The core move
Once the power and the efficiency story are clear, get *precise* about doctrine. Do not open with a
reform proposal. Open by locating which existing legal category the new technical facts strain, name
the exact load-bearing rationale or joint under stress, and say where the old framework stops fitting
cleanly. New facts don't automatically demand new law; they demand you find the joint.

## Diagnostic sub-moves (worked from the corpus)

**1. Ask what a doctrine is *really* load-bearing on.** A rule can survive new facts on one rationale
and shatter on another, so identify the rationale actually doing the work.
- *Indecent exposure in VR:* is the law protecting against **unease among passersby**, or is public
  indecency a **proxy for feared future physical attack**? If the latter, it largely dissolves when
  the exposer is only virtually present; if the former, it may survive. Which rationale is load-bearing
  *is* the case.

**2. Analogize to the nearest settled category, then say where the analogy breaks.**
- *A VR/AR headset's wrong directional cue* is like an **aeronautical chart** (a physical product, so
  potentially strict liability), not like the **Mushroom Encyclopedia** (pure thought and expression,
  not strictly liable) — "like a physical product, albeit one composed in large part of information."
  The placement decides the liability question; state precisely why it lands with the chart.

**3. Locate the fork the facts sit on.**
- *A nude avatar* sits exactly on the **speech/conduct** line: it is a display of video (like the
  protected drive-in-movie nudity — speech) *and* functionally aimed at emulating in-person presence
  (like public indecency — conduct). VR blurs an already-blurry line; name the blur rather than
  pretending the fact picks a side.

**4. Watch which doctrine plaintiffs will stretch, and whether it should bear the weight.**
- Operator liability for users' misconduct routes through **§230 immunity**, **negligent
  entrustment**, and **negligent supervision**; the right question is whether to draft operators into
  policing users, and at what cost to privacy and liberty. Impersonation/altered avatars route through
  **right of publicity**, **defamation/false light**, and **trademark dilution by tarnishment** — each
  a joint with its own load limits. Consent, the **"magic circle,"** and **factual impossibility in
  attempt** are the joints for in-game harms.

**5. Refuse to let realness stampede the category — either direction.** Do not let "it feels real"
drag perception into being treated as physical fact, and do not let "it's just a game / not real"
dismiss a genuine harm. The line must be re-earned on the new facts.

## The patent lesson: uniform-on-paper, industry-specific-in-fact, tailor via existing levers
Innovation is radically industry-specific (software ≠ pharma ≠ biotech), but the patent statute is
**nominally uniform**. The category failure is a "unitary" statute pretending industries are alike.
The fix is *not* bespoke per-industry statutes (which "invite the rent-seeking and balkanization that
specialized statutes would engender"); it is the **policy levers already in the doctrine** — the
flexible standards courts can apply differently as facts vary:
- **level of ordinary skill / PHOSITA**, **enablement**, **written description**, **nonobviousness**
  (macro levers that expressly treat industries differently, and micro levers that shift outcomes
  case-by-case).
Prefer working the lever already inside the standard over writing the statute not yet written; the
flexibility to vary the result on purpose is usually already sitting in the rule. Courts, not
Congress, are the better instrument because they can tailor without balkanizing.

## Evidence anchors (for auditing, not for verbatim reuse)
- Chart vs. Mushroom Encyclopedia; "like a physical product... composed in large part of information."
- Nude-avatar speech/conduct fork; the two rival rationales of indecent-exposure law.
- "policy levers permit patent law to take account of the technology-specific nature... without
  inviting the rent-seeking and balkanization that specialized statutes would engender."
