# Framework router and named constructs

Load this file when a request names a construct or spans multiple moves. It is a crosswalk, not a substitute for the depth modules.

## Three-move sequence

1. **Institutional/power map** — identify the regulatory model, relevant public and private governors, and the mechanism by which power operates or travels.
2. **Efficiency refusal** — concede actual gains, then ask what is optimized, for whom, who bears displaced costs, and whether automation complements or replaces human judgment.
3. **Doctrinal-mechanics diagnosis** — identify the claim, elements, load-bearing rationale, factual switch, competing analogies, existing policy lever, and residual gap.

Use all three for a hard technology-law problem. Do not force a move that adds nothing; preserve the order among moves actually used.

## Move 1 — comparative institutions

- **American market-driven regulatory model** — presumptive reliance on private firms, markets, permissionless innovation, and strong speech protection; its characteristic failure is concentrated private governance.
- **Chinese state-driven regulatory model** — technological development and markets remain instruments of state capacity, self-sufficiency, stability, and control; its characteristic failure is authoritarian surveillance and dependency.
- **European rights-driven regulatory model** — digital markets are constituted and constrained by binding public rules protecting individual and collective rights; its characteristic failures include compliance burden and enforcement gaps.
- **Horizontal battles** — contests among governments over technology, security, standards, supply chains, and the regulatory model that will organize markets.
- **Vertical battles** — contests between governments and technology companies over who can make, implement, and enforce operative rules.
- **Three carriers of digital power** — US influence chiefly through firms and markets; Chinese influence through infrastructure; EU influence through regulation.
- **Brussels Effect** — unilateral globalization of a domestic EU rule through market-access conditions and firms' incentives, without requiring treaty bargaining.
- **Five enabling conditions** — market size, regulatory capacity, stringent standards, inelastic targets, and non-divisibility. Treat them as a causal chain; size alone is not enough.
- **Legal / technical / economic non-divisibility** — separate compliance may be unavailable as law, impractical as architecture, or possible but costlier than one global standard.
- **De facto Brussels Effect** — firms globalize compliant conduct. **De jure Brussels Effect** — foreign legal systems adopt EU-style rules. One does not prove the other.
- **Upward harmonization** — market integration converges on relatively stringent member-state standards, often supported by high-regulation states seeking to protect both values and competitive position.
- **Digital Silk Road / infrastructure power** — installed networks, systems, finance, standards, training, and maintenance create a durable channel for Chinese influence.
- **Techno-democracy / techno-autocracy** — a regime-type frame for model rivalry; use only after examining actual contestability, surveillance, rights constraints, and private power.

## Move 2 — human expertise and automation

- **Four new laws of robotics** — AI and robotics should (1) complement professionals, not replace them; (2) not counterfeit humanity; (3) not intensify zero-sum arms races; and (4) indicate creators, controllers, and owners.
- **Complementarity** — automation transfers information and capability to responsible professionals while preserving authority over ends, exceptions, explanation, and learning. A nominal human checkpoint is not sufficient.
- **Counterfeit humanity** — engineered imitation induces mistaken attribution of human identity, attention, care, distress, or judgment while concealing sponsor and objective. Distinguish it from a merely usable or courteous interface.
- **Zero-sum arms race** — competitive pressure makes non-adoption costly, pushing all actors toward deployment despite collective risk.
- **Accountability map** — trace creator, deployer, controller, owner/beneficiary, responsible professional, and affected person across the lifecycle.
- **Two crises of expertise** — insulated technocratic overreach and indiscriminate anti-expert reaction. The response is democratized, contestable expertise, not proprietary automation.
- **Purposeful inefficiency / benefit of cost** — time, labor, deliberation, care, and payment can be constitutive value, capability, or social income rather than waste.
- **New behaviorism** — continuous data capture and stimulus-response optimization narrow education or work to measurable conduct and normalize surveillance.
- **Misrecognition as disrespect** — an automated system governs someone through a reductive identity, risk, or merit judgment they cannot meaningfully contest; harm exceeds prediction error.
- **Innovation governance** — public law, tax, procurement, finance, labor institutions, and professional standards shape what is automated; job training alone accepts rather than governs direction.
- **Cost disease critique** — rising expenditure in labor-intensive services may reflect wages, access, or better care. Do not infer inefficiency from slower productivity growth.
- **Pipeline problem** — removing junior or routine tasks can prevent future experts from developing the experience required to supervise, repair, and advance systems.
- **More AI versus better AI** — scale and substitution are not the same as accountable augmentation, maintenance, shared capability, and service of human purposes.

## Move 3 — doctrinal mechanics

- **Load-bearing rationale** — the legally grounded purpose or assumption actually supporting a doctrinal boundary. A new fact matters when it weakens that support.
- **Factual switch** — the smallest fact that changes classification or result: audiovisual versus haptic contact, opt-in versus default exposure, user versus platform creation, one patent versus thousands.
- **Analogy matrix** — compare actor/control, causal pathway, protected interest, consent, scale/persistence, territory, and doctrinal consequence; do not reason from surface resemblance.
- **Aeronautical chart versus Mushroom Encyclopedia** — distinguishes information integrated into physical action from expression a reader independently uses; functional causation, not the presence of words, drives the analogy.
- **Speech/conduct fork** — immersive representation may be protected communication and conduct-like unwanted presence at once; identify the protected interest and rationale rather than letting vividness decide.
- **Sensescape** — the user-specific perceptual environment a VR/AR system presents. Personalized filtering can support plural norms but does not erase first exposure, coercion, or non-filterable harm.
- **Bangladesh problem** — cross-border enforcement may be theoretically available but practically disproportionate for low-level virtual wrongs, creating pressure for private ordering and intermediary duties.
- **Magic circle** — the bounded play context supporting scoped consent to game acts. It is not blanket consent to hidden mechanics, impersonation, or external bodily, economic, and reputational consequences.
- **Order without (much) law** — architecture, defaults, filters, reputation, and private rules can govern where public enforcement is weak; this reallocates power rather than eliminating governance.
- **Innovation profile** — R&D cost/time, firm size, appropriability, spillovers, cumulative structure, patent-to-product ratio, lifecycle, risk, portfolios, standards, and alternatives to patent protection.
- **Patent theories as partial models** — classic incentive, prospect, competitive innovation, cumulative innovation, anticommons, and patent thickets have different factual predicates; none is universal.
- **Policy levers** — flexible doctrinal standards that can respond to relevant industry facts: PHOSITA, nonobviousness, enablement, written description, claim scope, equivalents, misuse, injunctions, and reasonable royalties.
- **Balkanization** — technology-specific statutes create category boundaries, capture, strategic classification, and obsolescence; test existing levers first.
- **Remedy separation** — validity, scope, liability, injunction, royalty base, and apportionment are different joints. Adjust the one producing the mismatch.

## Cross-move handoffs

| Finding | Hand off to |
| --- | --- |
| A jurisdiction protects a national champion because of interstate rivalry | Move 2: who captures efficiency/security gains and loses accountability; Move 3: identify the legal lever and limiting principle |
| A global firm standardizes around an EU rule | Move 2: distribution, entry, and responsibility; Move 3: separate legal scope from operational practice |
| AI replaces a professional function | Move 1: who gains governing power; Move 3: duties, standard of care, explanation, and remedy |
| A platform solves virtual harm through filters | Move 1: private governance; Move 2: whose burden and whose metric; Move 3: consent, first exposure, immunity, and direct duty |
| Patent protection is defended as innovation policy | Move 1: institutional competence and capture; Move 2: distribution and claimed inevitability; Move 3: industry profile and policy lever |

## Precision rules

- Preserve framework names but synthesize explanations; do not reproduce source passages.
- Do not turn ideal types into national stereotypes or a source-era statement of law into current authority.
- Distinguish descriptive mechanism, causal prediction, normative judgment, and legal conclusion.
- Attribute named frameworks to their sources when useful; never attribute this repository's combined sequence or a generated conclusion to a scholar.
